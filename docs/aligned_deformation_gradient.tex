\title{\textbf{Computing the Aligned Deformation Gradient $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$}\\[6pt]
\large Removing Arbitrary Mesh-Triangulation Orientations in First Piola--Kirchhoff and Projected Stress Post-Processing for Square and Triangular Lattices}
\author{MTM Lattice-Triangulation Documentation}
\date{November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In a 2D triangulated crystal mesh, each triangular element's raw deformation gradient $\mathbf{F}$ is defined relative to a single fixed canonical parent triangle. Because a unit cell consists of both lower-left/up ($\triangle$) and upper-right/down ($\triangledown$) triangles, and because CGAL Delaunay triangulation assigns counterclockwise vertex indices $(v_0, v_1, v_2)$ starting from an arbitrary corner, the raw deformation gradient $\mathbf{F}$ contains an arbitrary discrete reference-space transformation on the right. While the metric tensor $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{F}\T\mathbf{F}$ (after Lagrange reduction), the strain energy $W(\mathbf{C}_{\text{red}})$, and the Cauchy stress $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ are strictly invariant, the First Piola--Kirchhoff stress $\mathbf{P} = \partial W / \partial \mathbf{F}$ and the Projected Stress $\tau = \mathbf{m}\cdot(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{n})$ carry an explicit reference-space index and therefore flip sign or rotate columns between neighboring triangles. This document explains separately how $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$ is formulated for the \textbf{Square Lattice} and for the \textbf{Triangular (Hexagonal) Lattice} (both in the $\Z^2$ square reference frame and in the native equilateral reference frame), and how the entire post-processing stress pipeline follows directly from $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$ without altering any downstream formulas.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why the Raw Deformation Gradient $\mathbf{F}$ Depends on Triangle Labelling}
% ============================================================================

Let a triangular element have current deformed vertex positions $\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2 \in \R^2$ ordered counterclockwise ($\det[\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0,\;\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_0] > 0$). Denoting the two deformed edge vectors emanating from vertex $v_0$ by
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_1 = \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0, \qquad
  \mathbf{e}_2 = \mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_0, \qquad
  \mathbf{E} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{e}_1 & \mathbf{e}_2 \end{bmatrix} \in \R^{2\times 2},
\end{equation}
and the reference edge matrix of the canonical parent triangle by $\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}} = [\mathbf{E}_{1,\text{ref}},\; \mathbf{E}_{2,\text{ref}}]$, the raw element deformation gradient computed in \code{ElementTriangle2D::calculate\_deformation\_gradient} is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{E}\,\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{-1}.
  \label{eq:raw_F}
\end{equation}
In a finite-element mesh with dynamic Delaunay remeshing (\code{MeshGenerator::createTrianglesFromPoints} in \code{src/MeshGenerator.cpp}), triangles are destroyed and re-created during plastic avalanches, so individual triangles do not store their own initial node coordinates; every triangle is compared against the \textbf{single canonical parent triangle} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}$ via the fixed shape-derivative matrix $\texttt{dndx}$.

After CGAL constructs the Delaunay triangulation, \code{MeshGenerator::createTrianglesFromPoints} does \textbf{not} keep CGAL's raw vertex order. Instead, it explicitly reorders each triangle's three vertices $(v_0, v_1, v_2)$ in two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{\code{reorderForMaxAngle(v0, v1, v2)}}: Loops over all three corners of the triangle, computes the interior angle at each corner from the dot product of the two outgoing edges, and places the vertex with the \textbf{largest interior angle} first as \code{ordered[0]} ($v_0$). (If all angles are strictly acute, $< 75^\circ$, and the triangle is an isosceles grain-boundary kite with two equal legs, it places the apex between the two equal legs at \code{ordered[0]}.)
  \item \textbf{Counterclockwise (\code{cross\_product > 0}) enforcement}: Keeps \code{ordered[0]} first and orders the remaining two vertices as $(\code{ordered[0]}, \code{ordered[1]}, \code{ordered[2]})$ or $(\code{ordered[0]}, \code{ordered[2]}, \code{ordered[1]})$ so that $\det[\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2] > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
How this vertex-ordering rule interacts with the mesh triangles depends on whether the crystal is a \textbf{square lattice} or a \textbf{triangular (hexagonal) lattice}.

% ============================================================================
\section{Case 1: The Square Lattice}
% ============================================================================

In a square lattice, the canonical reference triangle is the bottom-left right-isosceles triangle with legs of length $h = a_0$ (or $h=1$ in normalized units):
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{sq}} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    h & 0 \\
    0 & h
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  (\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{sq}})^{-1} = \frac{1}{h}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 & 0 \\
    0 & 1
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Two discrete geometric ambiguities enter the raw deformation gradient $\mathbf{F} = \frac{1}{h}\mathbf{E}$ before any elastic deformation occurs:

\subsection{Square Ambiguity 1: The 4 Discrete $90^\circ$ Half-Cell Orientations ($\mathbf{R}_0, \mathbf{R}_1, \mathbf{R}_2, \mathbf{R}_3$)}
A unit square $[0,1]\times[0,1]$ has four corners and can be split along either diagonal ($\backslash$ or $/$) into four possible right-angled half-cell triangles whose $90^\circ$ corner sits at $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(1,1)$, or $(0,1)$. Even when `reorderForMaxAngle` places the origin $v_0$ directly at the $90^\circ$ corner of each triangle, walking counterclockwise produces four different raw deformation gradients rotated by $0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Bottom-left triangle ($\triangle$)} ($90^\circ$ corner at $v_0=(0,0) \to (1,0) \to (0,1)$, rotation $0^\circ$):
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{e}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \mathbf{F} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{R}_0.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Bottom-right triangle} ($90^\circ$ corner at $v_0=(1,0) \to (1,1) \to (0,0)$, rotation $90^\circ$):
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{e}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \mathbf{F} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{R}_1.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Top-right triangle ($\triangledown$)} ($90^\circ$ corner at $v_0=(1,1) \to (0,1) \to (1,0)$, rotation $180^\circ$):
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{e}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \mathbf{F} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{R}_2 = -\mathbf{I}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Top-left triangle} ($90^\circ$ corner at $v_0=(0,1) \to (0,0) \to (1,1)$, rotation $270^\circ$):
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{e}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \mathbf{F} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{R}_3.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Right-multiplying $\mathbf{F}$ by $\mathbf{R}_r\T$ ($r \in \{0,1,2,3\}$) rotates the local reference axes of the triangle by $-0^\circ, -90^\circ, -180^\circ, -270^\circ$, bringing all four half-square triangles back to $\mathbf{F}\mathbf{R}_r\T = +\mathbf{I}$.

\subsection{Square Ambiguity 2: The 3 Cyclic Counterclockwise Vertex Shifts ($\mathbf{M}_0, \mathbf{M}_1, \mathbf{M}_2$)}
In an undeformed or weakly deformed square lattice ($90^\circ, 45^\circ, 45^\circ$ triangles), \code{reorderForMaxAngle} deterministically places $v_0$ at the $90^\circ$ corner, so $\mathbf{M}_0 = \mathbf{I}$ is already selected. However, when a triangle is strongly sheared or plastically slipped (for instance, near a dislocation core, across a grain boundary, or when remeshing occurs after finite shear has opened one of the original $45^\circ$ corners into an obtuse angle $> 90^\circ$), \code{reorderForMaxAngle} places that obtuse corner at $v_0$ instead of the original lattice corner. Cycling which of the three corners is chosen as the origin $v_0$ (while keeping $(v_0, v_1, v_2)$ counterclockwise) transforms the edge matrix $\mathbf{E} = [\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2]$ by right-multiplication with a unimodular matrix $\mathbf{M}_p \in SL(2,\Z)$:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{No shift ($\mathbf{M}_0$)}, origin at $v_0=(0,0) \to v_1=(1,0) \to v_2=(0,1)$:
  \begin{equation}
    [\mathbf{e}_1,\; \mathbf{e}_2]
    = [\mathbf{e}_1,\; \mathbf{e}_2]
    \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}}_{\mathbf{M}_0}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{One cyclic shift ($\mathbf{M}_1$)}, origin shifted to $v_0'=(1,0) \to v_1'=(0,1) \to v_2'=(0,0)$:
  new edges are $\mathbf{e}_1' = \mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_1 = -\mathbf{e}_1 + \mathbf{e}_2$ and $\mathbf{e}_2' = \mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{x}_1 = -\mathbf{e}_1$:
  \begin{equation}
    [\mathbf{e}_1',\; \mathbf{e}_2']
    = [\mathbf{e}_1,\; \mathbf{e}_2]
    \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}}_{\mathbf{M}_1}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Two cyclic shifts ($\mathbf{M}_2$)}, origin shifted to $v_0''=(0,1) \to v_1''=(0,0) \to v_2''=(1,0)$:
  new edges are $\mathbf{e}_1'' = \mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{x}_2 = -\mathbf{e}_2$ and $\mathbf{e}_2'' = \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2 = \mathbf{e}_1 - \mathbf{e}_2$:
  \begin{equation}
    [\mathbf{e}_1'',\; \mathbf{e}_2'']
    = [\mathbf{e}_1,\; \mathbf{e}_2]
    \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}}_{\mathbf{M}_2}.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Note that $\det\mathbf{M}_0 = \det\mathbf{M}_1 = \det\mathbf{M}_2 = +1$, and $\mathbf{M}_0, \mathbf{M}_1, \mathbf{M}_2 \in SL(2,\Z)$ form a cyclic group of order $3$ ($\mathbf{M}_1^2 = \mathbf{M}_2$, $\mathbf{M}_1^3 = \mathbf{I}$, so $\mathbf{M}_1^{-1} = \mathbf{M}_2$ and $\mathbf{M}_2^{-1} = \mathbf{M}_1$).

\section{Case 2: The Triangular (Hexagonal) Lattice}
% ============================================================================

In a triangular (hexagonal) lattice with nearest-neighbor spacing $a_0$, every unit rhombus consists of an ``up'' equilateral triangle ($\triangle$) and a ``down'' equilateral triangle ($\triangledown$). Unlike the square lattice (where each right triangle has a unique $90^\circ$ corner that \code{reorderForMaxAngle} can identify at zero strain), \textbf{all three interior angles of an equilateral triangle are equal to $60^\circ$}. Consequently, floating-point roundoff or tiny elastic perturbations cause \code{reorderForMaxAngle} to pick \textbf{any of the 3 vertices} as $v_0$ even in a nearly undeformed crystal!

Combined with the two triangle types ($\triangle$ and $\triangledown$), the counterclockwise pair of edge vectors $(\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2)$ emanating from $v_0$ can take \textbf{6 discrete orientations} rotated by multiples of $60^\circ$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{E}^{(k)} = \mathbf{R}(k \cdot 60^\circ)\,\mathbf{E}^{(0)}, \qquad k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\},
\end{equation}
where $k \in \{0, 2, 4\}$ ($0^\circ, 120^\circ, 240^\circ$) are the 3 cyclic vertex orderings of an ``up'' triangle ($\triangle$) and $k \in \{3, 5, 1\}$ ($180^\circ, 300^\circ, 60^\circ$) are the 3 cyclic vertex orderings of a ``down'' triangle ($\triangledown$).

\begin{keyidea}[Why Does the Square Lattice Need $4 \times 3 = 12$ Tests, Instead of $6 \times 3 = 18$ in the Triangular Lattice?]
At first sight, since a square has $4$ orientations ($90^\circ$ steps) and $3$ cyclic vertex orderings ($4 \times 3 = 12$ tests), one might expect a triangular lattice with $6$ orientations ($60^\circ$ steps) and $3$ cyclic vertex orderings to require $6 \times 3 = 18$ tests. The reason it requires only \textbf{6} (and why the $18$ products repeat those $6$ matrices three times each) is geometric:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{In the Square Lattice ($4 \times 3 = 12$):} A half-square right triangle has \textbf{one $90^\circ$ corner and two $45^\circ$ corners} (two legs of length $a_0$ and one hypotenuse of length $\sqrt{2}a_0$). Because the triangle is \emph{not} equilateral, cycling its 3 vertices ($\mathbf{M}_0, \mathbf{M}_1, \mathbf{M}_2$) moves $v_0$ from the $90^\circ$ corner to a $45^\circ$ corner---which is \textbf{not a rigid rotation} of the triangle! Thus the $4$ rigid $90^\circ$ rotations of the right triangle ($\mathbf{R}_0, \mathbf{R}_1, \mathbf{R}_2, \mathbf{R}_3$) and the $3$ cyclic vertex shifts ($\mathbf{M}_0, \mathbf{M}_1, \mathbf{M}_2$) are completely independent, giving $4 \times 3 = 12$ distinct matrices.
  \item \textbf{In the Triangular Lattice ($2 \times 3 = 6$):} A unit $60^\circ$ rhombus contains only \textbf{2 equilateral triangles}: one ``up'' ($\triangle$, $0^\circ$) and one ``down'' ($\triangledown$, $180^\circ$). Because all 3 corners of an equilateral triangle have the exact same $60^\circ$ angle and side length $a_0$, \textbf{cycling the 3 vertices ($\mathbf{M}_0, \mathbf{M}_1, \mathbf{M}_2$) is already a rigid rotation by $0^\circ, 120^\circ, 240^\circ$}:
  \begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{tri}}\,\mathbf{M}_p\,(\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{tri}})^{-1} = \mathbf{R}(p \cdot 120^\circ), \qquad p \in \{0, 1, 2\}.
  \end{equation*}
  Combining the \textbf{2 triangle types} ($\triangle$ at $0^\circ$ and $\triangledown$ at $180^\circ$) with the \textbf{3 cyclic vertex orderings} ($0^\circ, 120^\circ, 240^\circ$) produces the $2 \times 3 = 6$ orientations ($0^\circ, 60^\circ, 120^\circ, 180^\circ, 240^\circ, 300^\circ$). If you multiply all $6$ rotations $\mathbf{R}(k\cdot 60^\circ)$ by the $3$ cyclic vertex shifts $\mathbf{R}(p\cdot 120^\circ)$, you get $\mathbf{R}((k + 2p)\cdot 60^\circ)$, which simply permutes the same $6$ matrices three times ($18 / 3 = 6$).
\end{itemize}
\end{keyidea}

In the MTM codebase (\code{ReferenceFrame.h} and \code{ReferenceFrame.cpp}), the triangular lattice can be run in two reference-frame modes, and \code{computeAlignedDeformationGradient} in \code{configuration\_saver.cpp} handles both:

\subsection{Mode 2A: Triangular Lattice in the $\Z^2$ Square Reference Frame (\code{ReferenceFrame::SquareZ2}, default \code{--triangular-reference=0})}
In the default $\Z^2$ reference frame, the canonical parent element remains the square right-isosceles triangle $\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{sq}} = h\,\mathbf{I}$ with $h = a_0 / (4/3)^{1/4}$ so that the unit-cell area is normalized to $1$. Under the hexagonal mapping
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}_{\text{hex}} = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{1/4}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 & 1/2 \\
    0 & \sqrt{3}/2
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{C}_{\text{ref}} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{hex}}\T \mathbf{F}_{\text{hex}}
  = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 & 1/2 \\
    1/2 & 1
  \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
the unit square $[0,h]\times[0,h]$ shears into a $60^\circ$ rhombus consisting of:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item The \textbf{lower-left half-square $\hat{\mathcal{T}}_+$ ($\mathbf{R}_0 = +\mathbf{I}$)}, which maps to the ``up'' equilateral triangle ($\triangle$).
  \item The \textbf{upper-right half-square $\hat{\mathcal{T}}_-$ ($\mathbf{R}_2 = -\mathbf{I}$)}, which maps to the ``down'' equilateral triangle ($\triangledown$).
\end{itemize}
Consequently, for any triangle in an undeformed triangular lattice (rotated by $\theta_0$), its raw deformation gradient measured against $\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{sq}}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F} = \pm\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_0)\,\mathbf{F}_{\text{hex}}\,\mathbf{M}_p^{-1},
  \qquad p \in \{0, 1, 2\},
\end{equation}
where $+1$ ($\mathbf{R}_0$) corresponds to $\triangle$ and $-1$ ($\mathbf{R}_2$) corresponds to $\triangledown$. Multiplying on the right by $\mathbf{M}_p\,\mathbf{R}_r\T$ with $r \in \{0, 2\}$ (so $\mathbf{R}_r\T = \pm\mathbf{I}$) immediately restores $\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{M}_p\,\mathbf{R}_r\T = +\mathbf{R}(\theta_0)\,\mathbf{F}_{\text{hex}}$ for every single $\triangle$ and $\triangledown$ triangle in the mesh! (Note that $r \in \{1, 3\}$ corresponds to pairing the two $60^\circ$ edges with a $90^\circ$ rotation of the square reference axes, which flips the sign of $F_{12}$ to the obtuse $-1/2$ well.)

\subsection{Mode 2B: Triangular Lattice in the Native Equilateral Reference Frame (\code{ReferenceFrame::Triangular}, \code{--triangular-reference=1})}
When \code{--triangular-reference=1} is enabled, the canonical parent triangle in \code{make\_reference\_setup} is the equilateral triangle with vertices $p_1=(0,0)$, $p_2=(a_0,0)$, $p_3=(\tfrac{1}{2}a_0, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a_0)$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{tri}} = a_0
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 & 1/2 \\
    0 & \sqrt{3}/2
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{C}_{\text{ref}} = \mathbf{I},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{F}_0 = \mathbf{I}.
\end{equation}
Because the reference triangle $\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{tri}}$ is itself equilateral, the 3 cyclic CCW vertex shifts $\mathbf{M}_p$ and the $\triangle / \triangledown$ sign flip $\pm\mathbf{I}$ conjugate into \textbf{exact $60^\circ$ orthogonal rotations} in the equilateral reference frame:
\begin{equation}
  \pm\,\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{tri}}\,\mathbf{M}_p\,(\mathbf{X}_{\text{ref}}^{\text{tri}})^{-1}
  = \mathbf{R}(k \cdot 60^\circ)
  = \begin{pmatrix}
    \cos(k\pi/3) & -\sin(k\pi/3) \\
    \sin(k\pi/3) & \cos(k\pi/3)
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}.
\end{equation}
Therefore, in \code{ReferenceFrame::Triangular}, the raw deformation gradient of an undeformed element is $\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{R}(\theta_0)\,\mathbf{R}(k \cdot 60^\circ)$, and aligning $\mathbf{F}$ simply tests the 6 hexagonal rotations $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}^{\text{(TriRef)}}
  = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{k \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5\}}
  \left\| \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{R}(k \cdot 60^\circ)\T - \mathbf{R}(\theta_0) \right\|_F^2.
  }
  \label{eq:f_aligned_tri_ref}
\end{equation}

\section{Unified Formula in $\Z^2$ Reference Mode and Summary Comparison}
% ============================================================================

In the default $\Z^2$ square-reference mode (\code{ReferenceFrame::SquareZ2}), a crystal in the Zanzotto potential can contain both square and triangular (hexagonal) phases simultaneously (as well as heavily sheared dislocation cores). We therefore use a \textbf{single unified formula} in \code{ReferenceFrame::SquareZ2} that aligns both square and triangular lattices without branching on lattice type.

In the Lagrange fundamental domain $0 \le 2\,C_{12} \le C_{11} \le C_{22}$:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item The \textbf{square phase} sits at the left boundary $F_{12} = 0$: $\mathbf{F}_{\text{sq}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
  \item The \textbf{triangular (hexagonal) phase} sits at the right boundary $F_{12} = +\tfrac{1}{2}s_0$: $\mathbf{F}_{\text{hex}} = s_0\begin{pmatrix} 1 & +1/2 \\ 0 & \sqrt{3}/2 \end{pmatrix}$.
\end{itemize}
By placing the target reference matrix $\mathbf{F}_{\text{target}}$ at the \textbf{midpoint} ($F_{12} = 1/4$) of the fundamental domain between the square and triangular wells,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}_{\text{target}} = \mathbf{R}(\theta_0)
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 & 1/4 \\
    0 & 1
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{R}(\theta_0) =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \cos\theta_0 & -\sin\theta_0 \\
    \sin\theta_0 & \cos\theta_0
  \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
both the square phase ($F_{12}=0$) and the acute triangular phase ($F_{12} \approx +1/2$) are at distance $\approx 1/4$ from $\mathbf{F}_{\text{target}}$, while the reflected obtuse triangular orientation ($F_{12} \approx -1/2$, $r \in \{1,3\}$) and unaligned square orientations ($F_{12}=\pm 1$) are at distance $\ge 3/4$.

Combining the $3$ cyclic triangle vertex permutations $\mathbf{M}_p$ ($p \in \{0,1,2\}$) with the $4$ discrete $90^\circ$ reference rotations $\mathbf{R}_r \in SL(2,\Z)$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{R}_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  \mathbf{R}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  \mathbf{R}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  \mathbf{R}_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
yields the unified $\Z^2$-reference formula used for both square and triangular lattices:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}^{\text{(SquareZ2)}} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\substack{p \in \{0,1,2\} \\ r \in \{0,1,2,3\}}}
  \left\| \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{M}_p\,\mathbf{R}_r\T - \mathbf{F}_{\text{target}} \right\|_F^2.
  }
  \label{eq:f_aligned_sq}
\end{equation}

\section{Why Nothing Downstream Needs to Change Once $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$ is Found}
% ============================================================================

\begin{keyidea}[Drop-In Replacement Property]
Because $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}} = \mathbf{F}\mathbf{N}$ where $\mathbf{N} \in SL(2,\Z)$ (in \code{SquareZ2} mode) or $\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{R}(k\cdot 60^\circ)\T$ (a point-group symmetry of the triangular lattice in \code{Triangular} mode), $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$ belongs to the \textbf{exact same lattice symmetry equivalence class} as $\mathbf{F}$. Therefore, replacing $\mathbf{F}$ by $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$ at the very start of the stress routine leaves the reduced metric $\mathbf{C}_{\text{red}}$, the strain energy $W$, and the Cauchy stress $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ mathematically unchanged, while automatically aligning the First Piola--Kirchhoff stress $\mathbf{P}_{\text{aligned}}$ and Projected Stress $\tau_{\text{aligned}}$.
\end{keyidea}

Once $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$ is computed via Eq.~\eqref{eq:f_aligned_tri_ref} or Eq.~\eqref{eq:f_aligned_sq}, the existing stress pipeline proceeds verbatim:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Aligned Right Cauchy--Green Metric Tensor}:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\T \mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}
    = \mathbf{N}\T \mathbf{F}\T \mathbf{F} \mathbf{N}
    = \mathbf{N}\T \mathbf{C} \mathbf{N}.
  \end{equation}

  \item \textbf{Lagrange Reduction to the Fundamental Domain}:
  In \code{SquareZ2} mode, applying \code{lagrange::reduce}$(\mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}})$ finds the unimodular reduction matrix $\mathbf{M} \in GL(2,\Z)$ that maps $\mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}}$ into the unique Lagrange-reduced metric $\mathbf{C}_{\text{red}}$:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{C}_{\text{red}} = \mathbf{M}\T \mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}} \mathbf{M}.
  \end{equation}
  For bulk elastic elements, $\mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}}$ is already in the fundamental domain, so $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{I}$. For plastically slipped elements (or elements at dislocation cores), $\mathbf{M}$ automatically strips the plastic lattice shear! (In \code{Triangular} reference mode without Lagrange reduction, $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{I}$ and $\mathbf{C}_{\text{red}} = \mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}}$.)

  \item \textbf{Constitutive Energy Derivative}:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{S}_{\text{red}} = \frac{1}{A_{\text{cell}}} \frac{\partial W(\mathbf{C}_{\text{red}})}{\partial \mathbf{C}_{\text{red}}}.
  \end{equation}

  \item \textbf{Aligned First Piola--Kirchhoff Stress Tensor}:
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \mathbf{P}_{\text{aligned}} = 2\,\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\,\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{S}_{\text{red}}\,\mathbf{M}\T.
    }
    \label{eq:P_aligned}
  \end{equation}

  \item \textbf{Cauchy Stress Tensor (Identical to Unaligned $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$)}:
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{aligned}}
    = \frac{1}{\det\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}} \mathbf{P}_{\text{aligned}} \mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\T
    = \frac{2}{\det\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}}
      (\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\mathbf{M})\,\mathbf{S}_{\text{red}}\,(\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\mathbf{M})\T
    = \boldsymbol{\sigma}.
    }
    \label{eq:cauchy_aligned}
  \end{equation}
  \emph{Proof of invariance:} Since $\mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}} = \mathbf{N}\T \mathbf{C} \mathbf{N}$ and $\det\mathbf{N} = 1$, both $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{C}_{\text{aligned}}$ reduce to the same canonical metric $\mathbf{C}_{\text{red}}$ in the fundamental domain, with $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{F}\mathbf{M}_{\text{orig}}$ (up to lattice point-group symmetries of $W$). Hence $(\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\mathbf{M})\mathbf{S}_{\text{red}}(\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}\mathbf{M})\T$ and $\det\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}} = \det\mathbf{F}\det\mathbf{N} = \det\mathbf{F}$ are completely invariant.

  \item \textbf{Lab-Frame First Piola--Kirchhoff and Projected Stress}:
  When the crystal is rotated by $\theta_0$ (rotate-and-cut CSL or fixed-BC supercell) and sheared in the lab frame with $\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}}(\alpha) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \alpha \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, rotating the reference index of $\mathbf{P}_{\text{aligned}}$ by $\mathbf{R}(\theta_0)\T$ gives the lab-frame First Piola--Kirchhoff tensor and the work-conjugate projected stress:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}_{\text{lab}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{aligned}}\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_0)\T, \qquad
    \boxed{
    \sigma_{\text{proj}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{lab}} : \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}}}{\partial \alpha}
    = (\mathbf{P}_{\text{lab}})_{12}
    = P_{11}^{\text{aligned}}\sin\theta_0 + P_{12}^{\text{aligned}}\cos\theta_0.
    }
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

% ============================================================================
\section{Lattice Orientation $\varphi$ and Lattice Rotation $\omega$}
\label{sec:lattice_rotation}
% ============================================================================

\begin{keyidea}[Independent of $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$]
The lattice orientation $\varphi$ and rotation $\omega$ are a separate measure. They do not use $\mathbf{F}_{\text{aligned}}$ or its target matrix: they are computed from the \emph{current} bond vectors of each element and compared with the ideal unit cell, so they do not depend on the reference frame (\code{SquareZ2} or \code{Triangular}), on the triangle labelling, on remeshing, or on the starting positions $\mathbf{X}_0$. Code: \code{measureLatticeOrientation} and \code{logLatticeRotation} in \code{src/configuration\_saver.cpp}.
\end{keyidea}

\subsection{Per element}

\paragraph{1. Current edge matrix.} With $\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{x}_e\,\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{N}$ built from the current atom positions, the current edge vectors are
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{E} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0 & \mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_0 \end{bmatrix}
  = \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{E}_{\text{ref}},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{E}_{\text{ref}}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} \nabla N_1\T \\ \nabla N_2\T \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
If $\det\mathbf{E} < 0$ (clockwise nodes) the two columns are swapped; elements with $\det\mathbf{F} \le 0$ are discarded.

\paragraph{2. Least-distorted lattice cell.} Let $\mathbf{B}$ be the ideal unit cell,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{B}_{\text{tri}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1/2 \\ 0 & \sqrt{3}/2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{B}_{\text{sq}} = \mathbf{I}.
\end{equation}
For every unimodular $\mathbf{M}$ with entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and $\det\mathbf{M} = 1$ (about 20 matrices, identity first),
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}\,\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{B}^{-1}, \qquad
  \mathbf{G}_n = \frac{\mathbf{G}}{\sqrt{\det\mathbf{G}}}, \qquad
  s(\mathbf{M}) = \big\| \mathbf{G}_n\T \mathbf{G}_n - \mathbf{I} \big\|_F^2,
\end{equation}
and the candidate with the smallest $s$ is kept. Removing the scale makes the choice depend on cell shape only. The distortion $d = \sqrt{s/2}$ is approximately the equivalent shear of the cell (VTK field \code{ElementLatticeDistortion}).

\paragraph{3. Orientation.} The rotation of the polar decomposition $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{U}$ has the closed form (2D, $\det\mathbf{G} > 0$)
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\varphi = \operatorname{atan2}\!\big(G_{21} - G_{12},\; G_{11} + G_{22}\big),}
\end{equation}
defined only modulo the lattice symmetry $\Theta_s = 60^\circ$ (triangular) or $90^\circ$ (square).

\paragraph{4. Rotation.} With $\theta$ the starting angle of the element's grain (majority grain label of its three nodes),
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\omega = \operatorname{wrap}_{\Theta_s}(\varphi - \theta)}
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{wrap}_{\Theta_s}$ into $(-\Theta_s/2, \Theta_s/2]$ (VTK field \code{ElementRotationAngleDeg}). A pristine grain gives $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{R}(\theta)$ exactly, hence $\omega = 0$, in both reference frames.

\paragraph{5. Reported orientation.} The VTK field \code{ElementLatticeOrientationDeg} stores
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_{\text{out}} = \theta + \omega,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\varphi$ folded around the grain's own starting angle rather than around $0^\circ$, so a grain whose starting angle lies near the fold stays continuous (values may lie outside $\pm\Theta_s/2$). Since the orientation is only defined modulo $\Theta_s$, any linear colour scale has a seam; the snapshot viewer therefore shows these fields with a cyclic colour map of period $\Theta_s$, on which $-\Theta_s/2$ and $+\Theta_s/2$ have the same colour.

Elements are excluded from the statistics below when their nodes belong to more than one grain (grain-boundary elements) or when $d > 0.35$ (dislocation cores, phase-transformed regions).

\subsection{Per grain and per load step (\code{lattice\_rotation\_log.csv})}

With $w_e$ the current element area and $k = 360^\circ/\Theta_s$ ($6$ triangular, $4$ square), the grain mean is the area-weighted circular mean
\begin{equation}
  \bar\omega_g = \frac{1}{k}\operatorname{atan2}\!\Big(\sum_{e\in g} w_e \sin k\omega_e,\; \sum_{e\in g} w_e \cos k\omega_e\Big),
\end{equation}
so that values near $\pm\Theta_s/2$ do not cancel. Successive steps are joined, $\bar\omega_g^{(n)} \leftarrow \bar\omega_g^{(n-1)} + \operatorname{wrap}_{\Theta_s}\!\big(\bar\omega_g - \bar\omega_g^{(n-1)}\big)$, so cumulative rotations beyond $\Theta_s/2$ stay continuous (\code{Grain$k$RotationDeg}); the current grain orientation is $\varphi_g = \theta_g + \bar\omega_g$ (\code{Grain$k$OrientationDeg}). The rigid rotation of the applied macroscopic deformation is
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{\text{applied}} = \operatorname{atan2}\!\big(F^{\text{ext}}_{21} - F^{\text{ext}}_{12},\; F^{\text{ext}}_{11} + F^{\text{ext}}_{22}\big) \;=\; -\arctan(\gamma/2) \text{ in simple shear},
\end{equation}
and \code{MeanRotationNetDeg} $= \bar\omega - \omega_{\text{applied}}$.

\paragraph{Physical meaning.} Slip along a lattice plane leaves bond directions unchanged, so dislocation glide alone gives $\omega = 0$; $\omega$ measures the turning of the lattice itself (elastic rotation, grain rotation, bending, kinking), as an EBSD map would.

\paragraph{Validation.} A crystal rotated by $17^\circ$ gives $|\omega| < 10^{-9}$ degrees at $\alpha = 0$ (triangular LJ in both reference frames, Zanzotto square); under simple shear the mean follows $-\arctan(\gamma/2)$ to within $10^{-3}$ degrees, and exactly for a periodic box.
