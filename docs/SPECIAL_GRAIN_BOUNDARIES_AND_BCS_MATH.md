# Mathematical Formulation of Special Grain Boundaries and Associated Boundary Conditions in the Mesoscopic Tensorial Model (MTM)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Purpose & Scope**
> This document establishes the complete mathematical foundation and algorithmic specification for constructing **special Coincidence Site Lattice (CSL) grain boundaries**, resolving **atomic-scale seam overlaps**, performing **energy-driven Delaunay topological surgery**, and enforcing the **two-stage boundary condition (BC) algebra** (**Relaxation Step BCs** vs. **Loading Step BCs & Indenter**) in the 2D Mesoscopic Tensorial Model (MTM). Every equation below maps directly to the C++ solver ([`MacroscopicGeometry.cpp`](../src/MacroscopicGeometry.cpp), [`zanzotto_examples.cpp`](../src/experiments/zanzotto_examples.cpp), [`configuration_saver.cpp`](../src/configuration_saver.cpp)) and the macOS studio ([`MacroscopicGeometryModels.swift`](../LatticeLauncher/Sources/MacroscopicGeometryModels.swift), [`GeometryBuilderView.swift`](../LatticeLauncher/Sources/GeometryBuilderView.swift)).

## 1. 2D Crystallographic Kinematics & Rotated Grain Lattices

### 1.1 Bravais Lattice Bases and Point-Group Symmetry
Let $a_0 > 0$ denote the fundamental equilibrium lattice parameter (with $a_0 = 1$ in normalized lattice units). A 2D Bravais lattice $\mathcal{L}_0 \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ is generated by integer linear combinations of two primitive basis vectors $\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^2$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_0 = \left\{ \mathbf{X}_{i,j} = i\,\mathbf{a}_1 + j\,\mathbf{a}_2 \;\middle|\; (i,j) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 \right\} = \mathbf{A}_0\,\mathbb{Z}^2, \qquad \mathbf{A}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{a}_1 & \mathbf{a}_2 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The MTM framework supports two fundamental 2D crystallographic symmetry classes:

1. **Square Lattice ($C_4 / D_4$ symmetry, `square`)**:
   $$
   \mathbf{a}_1 = a_0 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   \mathbf{a}_2 = a_0 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   \mathbf{A}_0^{\text{sq}} = a_0 \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   A_{\text{cell}}^{\text{sq}} = \det \mathbf{A}_0^{\text{sq}} = a_0^2.
   $$
   The rotational point group is $C_4$ (invariant under $90^\circ$ rotations), and together with mirror reflections across $\{10\}$ and $\{11\}$ planes forms the dihedral group $D_4$. Consequently, all physically distinct symmetric tilt misorientations $\Delta\theta$ lie in the fundamental interval $\Delta\theta \in [0^\circ, 90^\circ]$, with mirror-duality across $\Delta\theta = 45^\circ$.

2. **Triangular / Hexagonal Lattice ($C_6 / D_6$ symmetry, `triangular`)**:
   $$
   \mathbf{a}_1 = a_0 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   \mathbf{a}_2 = a_0 \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{1}{2} \\ \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   \mathbf{A}_0^{\text{tri}} = a_0 \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \tfrac{1}{2} \\ 0 & \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   A_{\text{cell}}^{\text{tri}} = \det \mathbf{A}_0^{\text{tri}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a_0^2.
   $$
   The rotational point group is $C_6$ (invariant under $60^\circ$ rotations), so all distinct symmetric tilt misorientations reside in $\Delta\theta \in [0^\circ, 60^\circ]$, with duality about $\Delta\theta = 30^\circ$.

For any grain $g \in \{1, 2, \dots, N_{\text{grains}}\}$ with crystallographic orientation angle $\theta_g \in \mathbb{R}$ and origin anchor $\mathbf{p}_g \in \mathbb{R}^2$, the rotated lattice basis is:

$$
\mathbf{A}_g = \mathbf{R}(\theta_g)\,\mathbf{A}_0, \qquad
\mathbf{R}(\theta_g) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_g & -\sin\theta_g \\ \sin\theta_g & \cos\theta_g \end{bmatrix} \in \mathrm{SO}(2),
$$

generating candidate atomic positions in grain $g$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_g(\theta_g, \mathbf{p}_g) = \left\{ \mathbf{X}_{i,j}^{(g)} = \mathbf{p}_g + i\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_g)\mathbf{a}_1 + j\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_g)\mathbf{a}_2 \;\middle|\; (i,j) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 \right\}.
$$

### 1.2 Bicrystal Grain Boundary Plane Geometry
In a macroscopic specimen domain $\Omega = [x_{\min}, x_{\min} + L_x] \times [y_{\min}, y_{\min} + L_y]$, a planar bicrystal interface $\Gamma_{\text{GB}}$ is parameterized by:
- Normalized pivot coordinates $(c_x, c_y) \in [0,1]^2$, yielding the physical pivot point:
  $$
  \mathbf{p}_{\text{GB}} = \begin{bmatrix} x_{\min} + c_x L_x \\ y_{\min} + c_y L_y \end{bmatrix}.
  $$
- Normal inclination angle $\phi_{\text{GB}}$ (`gb_angle_deg`, where $\phi_{\text{GB}} = 0^\circ$ defines a vertical grain boundary with normal along $+\hat{\mathbf{e}}_x$, and $\phi_{\text{GB}} = 90^\circ$ defines a horizontal grain boundary with normal along $+\hat{\mathbf{e}}_y$).

The orthonormal **Grain Boundary Coordinate Frame** $\{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{GB}}, \hat{\mathbf{t}}_{\text{GB}}\}$ is defined in [`MacroscopicGeometryConfig::generateDiscreteGrainLattice`](../src/MacroscopicGeometry.cpp) as:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{GB}}(\phi_{\text{GB}}) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\phi_{\text{GB}} \\ \sin\phi_{\text{GB}} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{\text{GB}}(\phi_{\text{GB}}) = \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\phi_{\text{GB}} \\ \cos\phi_{\text{GB}} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{GB}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{t}}_{\text{GB}} = 0.
$$

Every spatial point $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega$ has unique local GB coordinates $(s, \tau)$ representing the **signed normal distance** $s(\mathbf{x})$ from $\Gamma_{\text{GB}}$ and the **tangential distance** $\tau(\mathbf{x})$ along $\Gamma_{\text{GB}}$:

$$
s(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{GB}}) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{GB}}, \qquad
\tau(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{GB}}) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{t}}_{\text{GB}}.
$$

The domain $\Omega$ is partitioned into two half-crystals:
- **Grain 0 (Left / Lower half-crystal)**: $s(\mathbf{x}) \le 0$ with lattice orientation $\theta_1$,
- **Grain 1 (Right / Upper half-crystal)**: $s(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0$ with lattice orientation $\theta_2$.

For a **Symmetric Tilt Grain Boundary (STGB)** with half-tilt angle $\theta$ and total misorientation $\Delta\theta = 2\theta$:

$$
\theta_1 = \phi_{\text{GB}} - \theta = \phi_{\text{GB}} - \frac{\Delta\theta}{2}, \qquad
\theta_2 = \phi_{\text{GB}} + \theta = \phi_{\text{GB}} + \frac{\Delta\theta}{2}, \qquad
\Delta\theta = \theta_2 - \theta_1 = 2\theta.
$$

## 2. Coincidence Site Lattice (CSL) Theory & General $(m,n)$ Rationalizer

### 2.1 Diophantine Coincidence Condition on a 2D Square Lattice (Gaussian Integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$)
Why do arbitrary misorientation angles $\Delta\theta$ produce aperiodic, high-energy interfaces while discrete "special" angles produce periodic, low-energy cusps?

Consider two interpenetrating 2D square lattices $\mathcal{L}_1 = \mathbf{R}(-\theta)\mathbb{Z}^2 a_0$ and $\mathcal{L}_2 = \mathbf{R}(+\theta)\mathbb{Z}^2 a_0$ sharing a common origin at $\mathbf{p}_{\text{GB}} = \mathbf{0}$ on a vertical GB plane ($\phi_{\text{GB}} = 0^\circ$, where $\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{\text{GB}} = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_y$). A lattice site $\mathbf{p}_1 = a_0(m\,\hat{\mathbf{e}}_y + n\,\hat{\mathbf{e}}_x) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 a_0$ in the unrotated frame lies strictly along the vertical grain boundary axis ($\hat{\mathbf{e}}_y$) after rotation by $+\theta$ if and only if its $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_x$ component vanishes:

$$
\mathbf{R}(\theta)\begin{bmatrix} n a_0 \\ m a_0 \end{bmatrix} = a_0 \begin{bmatrix} n\cos\theta - m\sin\theta \\ n\sin\theta + m\cos\theta \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ L_{\text{CSL}} \end{bmatrix}.
$$

Setting the first component to zero yields the exact crystallographic **coprime integer generator law**:

$$
\tan\theta_{mn} = \frac{n}{m} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \theta_{mn} = \arctan\!\left(\frac{n}{m}\right), \qquad \Delta\theta_{mn} = 2\theta_{mn} = 2\arctan\!\left(\frac{n}{m}\right),
$$

where $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ are positive coprime integers ($\gcd(m,n) = 1$) with $1 \le n \le m$.

Evaluating $\cos\Delta\theta_{mn}$ and $\sin\Delta\theta_{mn}$ via double-angle trigonometric identities shows that the relative rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}(\Delta\theta_{mn}) = \mathbf{R}(2\theta_{mn})$ is a **rational orthogonal matrix** (equivalent to multiplication by $(m+in)/(m-in)$ in the ring of Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$):

$$
\mathbf{R}(\Delta\theta_{mn}) = \frac{1}{m^2 + n^2}
\begin{bmatrix}
m^2 - n^2 & -2mn \\
2mn & m^2 - n^2
\end{bmatrix} \in \mathrm{SO}(2, \mathbb{Q}).
$$

### 2.2 The Coincidence Index $\Sigma(m,n)$ and Parity Halving Rule
The **Coincidence Site Lattice** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CSL}} = \mathcal{L}_1 \cap \mathcal{L}_2$ is the sublattice of points common to both rotated crystals. Its index $\Sigma$ is defined as the ratio of the unit-cell area of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CSL}}$ to the primitive unit-cell area $a_0^2$ (equivalently, the reciprocal fraction of shared lattice sites):

$$
\Sigma_{\text{sq}}(m,n) = \frac{\det(\mathcal{L}_{\text{CSL}})}{a_0^2} = \frac{m^2 + n^2}{\gcd(m^2 + n^2,\, 2)}.
$$

> [!NOTE]
> **Why does $\gcd(m^2 + n^2, 2)$ appear in the denominator? (Odd vs. Even Parity & $90^\circ$ Duality)**
> Since $\gcd(m,n) = 1$, $m$ and $n$ cannot both be even. Thus two cases arise:
> 1. **Opposite Parity ($m+n$ is odd $\implies m^2+n^2$ is odd)**:
>    Here $\gcd(m^2+n^2, 2) = 1$, so $\Sigma(m,n) = m^2 + n^2$.
>    *Example*: $(m,n) = (2,1)$ gives $\Sigma = 2^2 + 1^2 = 5$, with $\theta_{2,1} = \arctan(1/2) \approx 26.565^\circ$ and $\Delta\theta_{2,1} \approx 53.130^\circ$ (the classic $\Sigma 5\,(210)$ boundary).
> 2. **Both Odd ($m$ and $n$ both odd $\implies m^2+n^2$ is even)**:
>    When $m$ and $n$ are both odd, $(m \pm n)$ are both even, so the centered vector $\frac{1}{2}\big((m+n)\mathbf{a}_1 + (m-n)\mathbf{a}_2\big)$ belongs to the integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2 a_0$. This halves the primitive CSL cell area by a factor of $\gcd(m^2+n^2, 2) = 2$:
>    $$
>    \Sigma(m,n) = \frac{m^2 + n^2}{2}.
>    $$
>    *Example*: $(m,n) = (3,1)$ has $m^2+n^2 = 10$, giving $\Sigma = 10/2 = 5$ at $\theta_{3,1} = \arctan(1/3) \approx 18.435^\circ$ and $\Delta\theta_{3,1} \approx 36.870^\circ$ (the dual $\Sigma 5\,(310)$ boundary).
>
> **Crystallographic Duality ($m' = m+n,\; n' = m-n$)**:
> Under the substitution $(m', n') = (m+n, m-n)$, we have:
> $$
> \tan\theta_{m',n'} = \frac{m-n}{m+n} = \frac{1 - n/m}{1 + n/m} = \tan(45^\circ - \theta_{mn}) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \Delta\theta_{m',n'} = 90^\circ - \Delta\theta_{mn},
> $$
> and $\Sigma(m',n') = \Sigma(m,n)$. Thus every special boundary at $\Delta\theta$ on a square lattice has a symmetry-related dual boundary of identical $\Sigma$ at $90^\circ - \Delta\theta$.

### 2.3 Extension to 2D Triangular / Hexagonal Lattices (Eisenstein Integers $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$)
On the 2D triangular lattice ($\mathbf{a}_1 = a_0(1,0)^T$, $\mathbf{a}_2 = a_0(1/2, \sqrt{3}/2)^T$, isomorphic to the Eisenstein integers $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$), a lattice vector $\mathbf{p}_{mn} = m\,\mathbf{a}_1 + n\,\mathbf{a}_2$ has squared Euclidean norm:

$$
\|\mathbf{p}_{mn}\|_2^2 = a_0^2\left(m^2 + mn + n^2\right).
$$

For coprime positive integers $(m,n)$ with $\gcd(m,n)=1$ and $1 \le n < m$, the symmetric tilt half-angle $\theta_{mn}^{\text{tri}}$, full misorientation $\Delta\theta_{mn}^{\text{tri}}$, and coincidence index $\Sigma_{\text{tri}}(m,n)$ are:

$$
\tan\theta_{mn}^{\text{tri}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}\,n}{2m + n}, \qquad
\Delta\theta_{mn}^{\text{tri}} = 2\arctan\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}\,n}{2m + n}\right), \qquad
\Sigma_{\text{tri}}(m,n) = \frac{m^2 + mn + n^2}{\gcd(m^2 + mn + n^2,\, 3)}.
$$

When $m - n \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, $m^2 + mn + n^2$ is divisible by $3$, and the centered hexagonal vector $\frac{1}{3}\big((2m+n)\mathbf{a}_1 + (n-m)\mathbf{a}_2\big) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ reduces the CSL unit cell area by a factor of $3$. For example:
- $(m,n) = (2,1)$: $m^2+mn+n^2 = 7$, $\gcd(7,3)=1 \implies \Sigma 7$ at $\Delta\theta = 2\arctan(\sqrt{3}/5) \approx 38.213^\circ$ (and its $60^\circ - \Delta\theta$ dual at $21.787^\circ$ via $(m,n)=(3,1)$ where $(9+3+1)$ is $\Sigma 13$, or $(m',n')=(m+n, m-n)=(3,1)$ halved by $3$: $(3^2+3\cdot 1+1^2)=13$, whereas $(m',n')=(m+2n, m-n)=(4,1)$ has $16+4+1=21$, $21/3 = \Sigma 7$ at $\Delta\theta = 21.787^\circ$).
- $(m,n) = (3,1)$: $m^2+mn+n^2 = 13 \implies \Sigma 13$ at $\Delta\theta \approx 27.796^\circ$ (and dual $(5,2)$ with $25+10+4=39$, $39/3 = \Sigma 13$ at $\Delta\theta \approx 32.204^\circ$).

### 2.4 Structural Repeat Period $L_{\text{CSL}}$, Interplanar Layer Spacing $d_{mn}$, and Commensurate Box Sizing
From the rotated vector equation $\mathbf{R}(\theta_{mn})\,[n a_0,\, m a_0]^T = [0,\, L_{\text{CSL}}]^T$ on the square lattice, the exact **structural repeat period** along the grain boundary plane $\Gamma_{\text{GB}}$ is:

$$
L_{\text{CSL}}(m,n) = a_0 \sqrt{m^2 + n^2}.
$$

Because the 2D unit-cell area $a_0^2$ is preserved under orthogonal rotation, the area spanned by one structural period $L_{\text{CSL}}$ along the GB and one interplanar spacing $d_{mn}$ normal to the GB equals $a_0^2$:

$$
L_{\text{CSL}}(m,n) \cdot d_{mn} = a_0^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad d_{mn} = \frac{a_0}{\sqrt{m^2 + n^2}} = \frac{a_0^2}{L_{\text{CSL}}(m,n)}.
$$

To prevent artificial geometric frustration where the grain boundary terminates at the top and bottom specimen edges ($y = y_{\min}$ and $y = y_{\min} + L_y$), the specimen dimension along the GB is quantized to an integer multiple $K_{\text{CSL}} \ge 2$ of $L_{\text{CSL}}(m,n)$ in [`MicrostructureConfigModel::cslCandidates`](../LatticeLauncher/Sources/MacroscopicGeometryModels.swift):

$$
K_{\text{CSL}} = \max\!\left(2,\; \operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{N_{\text{target}}\,a_0}{L_{\text{CSL}}(m,n)}\right)\right), \qquad
N^{\text{CSL}}(m,n) = \operatorname{round}\!\left(K_{\text{CSL}}\sqrt{m^2 + n^2}\right).
$$

## 3. Microscopic Degrees of Freedom, Seam Overlap Resolution & Analytical Pre-Opening

Macroscopic crystallography fixes $(\theta_1, \theta_2, \phi_{\text{GB}})$ (three macroscopic parameters in 2D). However, the actual atomic structure of the grain boundary core depends critically on **two microscopic translational degrees of freedom** $(\delta_\parallel, \delta_\perp)$ and the **seam overlap resolution operator**.

### 3.1 Coincidence Capture Band and Rigid Half-Crystal Translations
When generating discrete lattice points for a bicrystal in [`MacroscopicGeometryConfig::generateDiscreteGrainLattice`](../src/MacroscopicGeometry.cpp), both Grain 0 and Grain 1 are anchored directly at the grain boundary pivot $\mathbf{p}_{\text{GB}}$ so that $(0,0)$ is an exact shared coincidence site:

1. **Grain 0 Generation ($s(\mathbf{X}) \le +\epsilon_{\text{band}}$)**:
   $$
   \mathbf{X}_{i,j}^{(0)} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{GB}} + i\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_1)\mathbf{a}_1 + j\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_1)\mathbf{a}_2, \qquad s(\mathbf{X}_{i,j}^{(0)}) \le +\epsilon_{\text{band}},
   $$
   where $\epsilon_{\text{band}} = 0.02\,a_0$ is a symmetric **coincidence capture band** around $s = 0$.
2. **Grain 1 Generation ($s(\mathbf{X}) \ge -\epsilon_{\text{band}}$) with Microscopic Shifts $(\delta_\parallel, \delta_\perp)$**:
   Before applying the microscopic shift, candidate sites $\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{i,j}^{(1)} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{GB}} + i\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_2)\mathbf{a}_1 + j\,\mathbf{R}(\theta_2)\mathbf{a}_2$ are tested against the unshifted boundary plane $s(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{i,j}^{(1)}) \ge -\epsilon_{\text{band}}$, and then translated by:
   $$
   \mathbf{X}_{i,j}^{(1)} = \tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{i,j}^{(1)} + \delta_\parallel a_0\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{\text{GB}} + \delta_\perp a_0\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{GB}},
   $$
   where:
   - $\delta_\parallel$ (`gb_shift_tangent_a0`) is the rigid-body **tangential shift** of Grain 1 parallel to $\Gamma_{\text{GB}}$,
   - $\delta_\perp$ (`gb_normal_gap_a0`) is the rigid-body **normal expansion / pre-opening gap** across $\Gamma_{\text{GB}}$.

---

### 3.2 Spatial-Hash Cross-Grain Seam Overlap Resolution
Near the interface $s(\mathbf{X}) \approx 0$, rotating two lattices independently produces pairs of atoms $(p \in G_0, q \in G_1)$ whose Euclidean separation $r_{pq} = \|\mathbf{X}_p - \mathbf{X}_q\|_2$ is smaller than the physical atomic core exclusion distance $d_{\min}\,a_0$ (default $d_{\min} = 0.45$). If left unresolved, a pair with $r_{pq} \ll a_0$ generates degenerate sliver triangles with $\det\mathbf{F}_e \to 0$ and infinite elastic energy.

The **Seam Overlap Filter** ([`MacroscopicGeometryConfig::filterGrainSeamOverlaps`](../src/MacroscopicGeometry.cpp)) resolves all cross-grain conflicts deterministically in $\mathcal{O}(N)$ time:

1. **Spatial Hashing & Candidate Pair Extraction**:
   Points are bucketed into uniform grid cells of side length $h_{\text{cell}} = d_{\min} a_0$. Every cross-grain pair $(p, q)$ with $g_p \neq g_q$ and $\|\mathbf{X}_p - \mathbf{X}_q\|_2 < d_{\min} a_0$ is collected:
   $$
   \mathcal{P}_{\text{overlap}} = \left\{ (p, q) \;\middle|\; p < q,\; g_p \neq g_q,\; \|\mathbf{X}_p - \mathbf{X}_q\|_2 < d_{\min} a_0 \right\}.
   $$
2. **Distance-Sorted Greedy Resolution**:
   $\mathcal{P}_{\text{overlap}}$ is sorted in ascending order of distance $r_{pq}$ (closest pairs first, starting with exact CSL coincidence pairs at $r_{pq} \approx \delta_\perp a_0$). For each pair $(p, q)$ where neither $p$ nor $q$ has been removed yet:
   - **`DeleteSecond` Policy (`SeamOverlapResolution::DeleteSecond`)**:
     Retains $\mathbf{X}_p$ (in Grain 0) and marks $q$ (in Grain 1) as removed.
   - **`MergeMidpoint` Policy (`SeamOverlapResolution::MergeMidpoint`, Default)**:
     Computes the symmetric midpoint:
     $$
     \mathbf{X}_{\text{mid}} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{X}_p + \mathbf{X}_q\right).
     $$
     Before moving $p \to \mathbf{X}_{\text{mid}}$, a **secondary crowding guard** checks all surviving neighbors $k \notin \{p, q\}$ within the $3 \times 3$ spatial hash neighborhood against a safety radius $d_{\text{safe}} = 0.85\,d_{\min} a_0$:
     $$
     \forall k \notin \{p, q\}:\quad \|\mathbf{X}_k - \mathbf{X}_{\text{mid}}\|_2 \ge d_{\text{safe}}.
     $$
     - If the crowding guard passes, $\mathbf{X}_p \leftarrow \mathbf{X}_{\text{mid}}$ and $q$ is removed. This places shared structural-unit vertices **strictly on the mirror symmetry plane** of the symmetric tilt boundary.
     - If moving $p$ to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{mid}}$ would crowd an adjacent third atom $k$, the filter safely falls back to retaining $\mathbf{X}_p$ in place and removing $q$.

### 3.3 Exact Derivation of the Analytical Normal Pre-Opening Gap $\delta_\perp(m,n)$
Let $d_{mn} = a_0 / \sqrt{m^2 + n^2}$ be the interplanar spacing of atomic layers parallel to the symmetric tilt boundary. In the unshifted configuration ($\delta_\perp = 0$), the $k$-th atomic plane in Grain 0 sits at $s = -k\,d_{mn}$ and its mirror image in Grain 1 sits at $s = +k\,d_{mn}$. Across the boundary, mirror-symmetric atoms on layer $k$ are separated by:

$$
r_k = 2k\,d_{mn}, \qquad k \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}.
$$

All layers $k$ with $r_k < d_{\min} a_0$ are fused onto the boundary plane ($s=0$) by the `MergeMidpoint` filter. The **first surviving (unmerged) cross-boundary layer pair** therefore has index:

$$
k_{\text{first}}(m,n) = \max\!\left(1,\; \left\lceil \frac{d_{\min} a_0}{2\,d_{mn}} \right\rceil\right) = \max\!\left(1,\; \left\lceil \frac{d_{\min}\sqrt{m^2+n^2}}{2} \right\rceil\right),
$$

and its unshifted cross-boundary bond length is:

$$
r_{\text{first}}(m,n) = 2\,k_{\text{first}}(m,n)\,d_{mn} = \frac{2\,k_{\text{first}}(m,n)}{\sqrt{m^2 + n^2}}\,a_0.
$$

To initialize the grain boundary near its natural zero-pressure equilibrium volume and avoid starting inside the steep repulsive wall of the Zanzotto potential, we define the **analytical pre-opening normal gap**:

$$
\delta_\perp(m,n) = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{r_{\text{target}} - r_{\text{first}}(m,n)}{a_0},\; 0.0,\; 0.42\right), \qquad r_{\text{target}} = 0.94\,a_0.
$$

Adding $\delta_\perp(m,n) a_0\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{GB}}$ increases the initial cross-seam separation of the first unmerged layer pair from $r_{\text{first}}$ to $r_{\text{first}} + \delta_\perp a_0 \approx 0.94\,a_0$, placing all interfacial Delaunay triangles smoothly inside the harmonic basin of attraction around $\det\mathbf{F}_e \approx 1$.

## 4. Constrained Delaunay Triangulation, MTM Energy & Topological Core Surgery

### 4.1 Finite-Element Kinematics and $\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$-Invariant Strain Energy
Given the overlap-filtered reference point cloud $\mathcal{X}_0 = \{\mathbf{X}_1, \dots, \mathbf{X}_N\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, a 2D Delaunay triangulation $\mathcal{T}$ is constructed and pruned by:
1. Removing any triangle whose centroid falls inside an active geometric cutout (`Circle`, `Ellipse`, `Rectangle`, `EdgeNotch`, `Polygon`), and
2. Applying a **maximum chord-length filter** $l_{\max} = 2.60\,a_0$: any triangle with an edge $\|\mathbf{X}_a - \mathbf{X}_b\|_2 > 2.60\,a_0$ spanning across a non-convex notch, free surface step, or grain boundary ledge is removed.

Each element $e \in \mathcal{T}$ with vertices $(i_0, i_1, i_2)$ has deformed edge vectors $\mathbf{d}_1 = \mathbf{x}_{i_1} - \mathbf{x}_{i_0}$, $\mathbf{d}_2 = \mathbf{x}_{i_2} - \mathbf{x}_{i_0}$ and reference shape matrix $\mathbf{D}_{0,e}$, defining the piecewise-constant **deformation gradient** $\mathbf{F}_e$ and **Cauchy-Born right Cauchy-Green metric tensor** $\mathbf{C}_e$:

$$
\mathbf{F}_e = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{d}_1 & \mathbf{d}_2 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{D}_{0,e}^{-1}, \qquad
\mathbf{C}_e = \mathbf{F}_e^T \mathbf{F}_e \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(2).
$$

Because a crystal lattice is invariant under the discrete modular group $\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) = \{\mathbf{M} \in \mathbb{Z}^{2\times 2} \mid |\det\mathbf{M}| = 1\}$ of lattice-invariant shears, the strain energy density $\Phi(\mathbf{C}_e)$ is evaluated by first mapping $\mathbf{C}_e$ into the fundamental **Lagrange-reduced domain** $\mathcal{D}_{\text{red}}$ via Gauss-Lagrange lattice basis reduction (`lagrange::reduce(C)`):

$$
\mathbf{C}_{\text{red},e} = \mathbf{M}_e^T \mathbf{C}_e \mathbf{M}_e \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{red}}, \qquad \mathbf{M}_e \in \mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}),
$$

where $\mathcal{D}_{\text{red}} = \left\{ \mathbf{C} \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(2) \;\middle|\; 2|C_{12}| \le \min(C_{11}, C_{22}) \right\}$. The total MTM potential energy over all active elements $\mathcal{T}_{\text{act}}$ is:

$$
E_{\text{MTM}}\big(\{\mathbf{x}_i\},\, \mathcal{T}_{\text{act}}\big) = \sum_{e \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{act}}} \frac{A_{0,e}}{A_{\text{cell}}} \Phi_{\text{Zanzotto}}(\mathbf{C}_{\text{red},e}) + E_{\text{indenter}}\big(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}, \alpha\big),
$$

and the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor in element $e$ is pulled back through $\mathbf{M}_e$:

$$
\mathbf{P}_e = \frac{2}{A_{\text{cell}}}\,\mathbf{F}_e\,\mathbf{M}_e\,\frac{\partial \Phi_{\text{Zanzotto}}(\mathbf{C}_{\text{red},e})}{\partial \mathbf{C}_{\text{red}}}\,\mathbf{M}_e^T.
$$

### 4.2 Energy-Driven Delaunay Core Surgery (`perform_remeshing_loop_reduction`)
To allow true bond switching in the GB core without introducing non-variational energy jumps, [`perform_remeshing_loop_reduction`](../src/experiments/zanzotto_examples.cpp) executes an **energy-monotone alternating minimization** over positions $\{\mathbf{x}_i\}$ and Delaunay topology $\mathcal{T}$. A candidate Delaunay retriangulation $\mathcal{T}_{\text{cand}}$ is accepted if and only if:
1. **Non-trivial topological change**: $\Delta N_{\text{tris}} = |\mathcal{T}_{\text{cand}} \triangle \mathcal{T}_k| > 0$,
2. **Acyclicity**: the sorted canonical signature of $\mathcal{T}_{\text{cand}}$ has not been visited in any prior surgery pass of the current step, and
3. **Strict thermodynamic descent**:
   $$
   \Delta E_{\text{surgery}} = E(\mathbf{x}_{\text{trial}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{cand}}) - E(\mathbf{x}_{\text{old}}, \mathcal{T}_k) < -\epsilon_{\text{rel}}\,|E(\mathbf{x}_{\text{old}}, \mathcal{T}_k)|, \qquad \epsilon_{\text{rel}} = 10^{-6}.
   $$
   If $\Delta E_{\text{surgery}} \ge -\epsilon_{\text{rel}}|E_{\text{old}}|$, the topological flip is rejected and the solver rolls back to $(\mathbf{x}_{\text{old}}, \mathcal{T}_k)$.

## 5. Mathematics of Associated Boundary Conditions (Relaxation Step vs. Loading Step)

### 5.1 Independent $(x, y)$ Nodal Kinematic Classification (`node_bc_role` $\in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$)
In 2D, each node $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ possesses two translational degrees of freedom, $\mathbf{u}_i = (u_{x,i}, u_{y,i})^T = \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{X}_{0,i}$. [`MacroscopicGeometryConfig::classifyBoundaryNodes`](../src/MacroscopicGeometry.cpp) evaluates a boolean state tuple $(\ell_{x,i}, \ell_{y,i}, d_{x,i}, d_{y,i}) \in \{0,1\}^4$ and velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_i = (v_{x,i}, v_{y,i})^T$ by sequentially composing all active `BoundaryZoneRule` entries matching node $i$:

$$
\text{role}(i) =
\begin{cases}
0 \quad (\text{Free}): & \ell_{x,i} = 0 \;\land\; \ell_{y,i} = 0 \qquad (\text{both } u_{x,i}, u_{y,i} \text{ free}), \\[4pt]
1 \quad (\text{Fixed}): & \ell_{x,i} = 1 \;\land\; \ell_{y,i} = 1 \;\land\; \neg(d_{x,i} \lor d_{y,i}) \qquad (u_{x,i} = 0,\; u_{y,i} = 0), \\[4pt]
2 \quad (\text{Driven}): & \ell_{x,i} = 1 \;\land\; \ell_{y,i} = 1 \;\land\; (d_{x,i} \lor d_{y,i}) \qquad (\mathbf{u}_i(\alpha) = \alpha\,\mathbf{v}_i), \\[4pt]
3 \quad (\text{RollerY / X-Locked}): & \ell_{x,i} = 1 \;\land\; \ell_{y,i} = 0 \qquad (u_{x,i}(\alpha) = \alpha v_{x,i},\; u_{y,i} \text{ free}), \\[4pt]
4 \quad (\text{RollerX / Y-Locked}): & \ell_{x,i} = 0 \;\land\; \ell_{y,i} = 1 \qquad (u_{x,i} \text{ free},\; u_{y,i}(\alpha) = \alpha v_{y,i}).
\end{cases}
$$

---

### 5.2 Subspace Projection Operator $\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}}$ and Preconditioned Hessian Restriction
Every node with at least one free degree of freedom ($\text{role}(i) \in \{0, 3, 4\}$) is included in the active solver index map `interior_mapping` ($k \in \{0, \dots, n_{\text{vars}}-1\}$), where the ALGLIB L-BFGS state vector $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{2n_{\text{vars}}}$ stores $z_k = x_{i(k)}$ and $z_{n_{\text{vars}} + k} = y_{i(k)}$.

The orthogonal **Boundary Subspace Projection Operator** $\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}} = \operatorname{diag}(p_0, \dots, p_{2n_{\text{vars}}-1})$ is defined by:

$$
p_k = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \text{role}(i(k)) = 3, \\ 1 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad
p_{n_{\text{vars}}+k} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \text{role}(i(k)) = 4, \\ 1 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
$$

satisfying $\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}}^2 = \mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}}^T$. When using the sparse CHOLMOD stiffness preconditioner $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{2n_{\text{vars}} \times 2n_{\text{vars}}}$, the preconditioned operator restricted to the admissible roller subspace $\mathcal{V}_{\text{BC}} = \operatorname{im}(\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}})$ is:

$$
\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}}\,\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}} + \big(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}}\big).
$$

Because $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ is block-diagonal with respect to the decomposition $\mathbb{R}^{2n_{\text{vars}}} = \mathcal{V}_{\text{BC}} \oplus \mathcal{V}_{\text{BC}}^\perp$ and acts as the identity on $\mathcal{V}_{\text{BC}}^\perp = \ker(\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}})$, its inverse commutes with $\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}}$ on projected gradients:

$$
\mathbf{d} = -\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-1}\big(\mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}}\nabla E\big) \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{BC}} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{P}_{\text{BC}})\,\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{0},
$$

ensuring that constrained roller coordinates remain invariant to machine precision while retaining superlinear preconditioned convergence along the free coordinates.

### 5.3 Mathematical Proof of the Stage 1 Relaxation Step BCs (`GB-Frame Rollers`)
For a vertical bicrystal ($\phi_{\text{GB}} = 0^\circ$), the default **Relaxation Step BC** preset (`GB Roller (Default)`) assigns:
- **Left Edge**: `RollerY` ($u_x = 0$, $u_y$ free),
- **Right Edge**: `FreeSurface` ($\boldsymbol{\sigma}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_x = \mathbf{0}$),
- **Top & Bottom Edges**: `RollerX` ($u_y = 0$, $u_x$ free).

This configuration satisfies three simultaneous theorems:
1. **Elimination of Rigid-Body Modes**: Any 2D rigid-body motion $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}) = (c_x - \omega y,\, c_y + \omega x)^T$ satisfying $u_x(x_{\min}, y) = 0$ and $u_y(x, y_{\min}) = 0$ must have $c_x - \omega y = 0\;\forall y \implies c_x = \omega = 0$, and $c_y + \omega x = 0 \implies c_y = 0$.
2. **Zero Macroscopic Normal Stress ($\sigma_{xx}^{\text{eq}} = 0$)**: Because the Right Edge is traction-free and the Top/Bottom edges have zero horizontal shear traction ($P_{xy} = 0$ since $u_x$ is free), global horizontal force balance over $\Omega$ yields:
   $$
   \int_{x = x_{\min}} P_{xx}\,dy = \int_{x = x_{\max}} P_{xx}\,dy = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \langle P_{xx} \rangle_\Omega = 0.
   $$
   Thus the grain boundary expands or contracts freely to its exact thermodynamic excess volume $\Delta u_\perp^{\text{eq}}$ without compressing the adjacent bulk grains.
3. **Suppression of End Wedge Splitting**: Locking $u_y = 0$ on Top and Bottom enforces mirror symmetry across $y = y_{\min}$ and $y = y_{\max}$, eliminating the free-surface thermal groove singularity that would otherwise unzip a finite bicrystal at its terminations.

## 6. Two-Stage Handover Mathematics: Reference Re-Anchoring ($\mathbf{X}_0 \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{\text{eq}}$) & Stage 2 Loading

When transitioning from **Stage 1 (`Relaxation Step BCs`)** to **Stage 2 (`Loading Step BCs & Indenter`)**, the solver executes the **Reference Handover Operator**:

$$
\mathbf{X}_{0,i} \longleftarrow \mathbf{x}_{\text{eq},i} \qquad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, N\},
$$

while preserving the crystallographic element shape matrices $\mathbf{D}_{0,e}$. Consequently:
- Every Stage 2 Fixed ($\text{role}=1$) or Driven ($\text{role}=2$) node follows $\mathbf{x}_i^{\text{Stage 2}}(\alpha) = \mathbf{x}_{\text{eq},i} + \alpha\,\mathbf{v}_i$, eliminating the spurious boundary snap jump $\Delta\mathbf{u}_{\text{snap},i} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{unrelaxed},i} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{eq},i}$ that would otherwise occur at $\alpha = 0^+$.
- When the Stage 2 Nanoindenter is enabled, the top surface contact reference $y_{\text{top}}^{\text{eq}} = \max_{|x_{\text{eq},i} - x_{\text{tip}}| \le w_{\text{zone}}} y_{\text{eq},i}$ is re-measured directly from $\mathbf{x}_{\text{eq}}$ so the indenter engages the true relaxed grain boundary surface profile.

## 7. Grain Boundary Excess Energy Functional $\gamma_{\text{GB}}(\Delta\theta)$

Excluding an outer perimeter collar of width $w_{\text{collar}} = 4\,a_0$ defines the interior gauge region $\Omega_{\text{int}} = [x_{\min}+w_{\text{collar}}, x_{\max}-w_{\text{collar}}] \times [y_{\min}+w_{\text{collar}}, y_{\max}-w_{\text{collar}}]$ and the collar-excluded specific grain boundary excess energy:

$$
\gamma_{\text{GB}}^{\text{int}}(\Delta\theta) = \frac{1}{L_{\text{GB}}^{\text{int}}} \sum_{\substack{e \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{eq}} \\ \mathbf{x}_{\text{centroid},e} \in \Omega_{\text{int}}}} \frac{A_{0,e}}{A_{\text{cell}}} \Phi_{\text{Zanzotto}}(\mathbf{C}_{\text{red},e}),
$$

which captures both the low-angle Read-Shockley logarithmic regime $\gamma_{\text{GB}}(\Delta\theta) = E_0\Delta\theta(A_c - \ln\Delta\theta)$ and the secondary dislocation cusps around special low-$\Sigma$ CSL misorientations $\Delta\theta_{mn}$.
